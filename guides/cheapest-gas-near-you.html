How to Find the Cheapest Gas Near You

Gas prices can differ by 50 cents a gallon or more between two stations a few minutes apart. Knowing where — and when — to fill up can save a typical driver several hundred dollars a year.

Start with a gas price app

The fastest way to find cheap gas is a price-comparison app. Drivers report prices at stations near them, and the app shows a map of the cheapest options. Before you drive somewhere special for cheap gas, check the distance: driving 10 extra miles to save 10 cents a gallon usually costs more than it saves.

Look away from the highway

Stations right off a highway exit pay high rent and serve drivers who have no choice — so they charge more. Stations a mile or two into town, near supermarkets, or on the outskirts are often much cheaper. If you can, fill up before you get on the highway, not at the first exit.

Time your fill-ups

Prices often rise before weekends and holidays, when more people travel. Filling up on a Monday or Tuesday morning is often cheaper than Friday afternoon. Prices also tend to change early in the morning, so an early fill-up can catch the lower price.

Use loyalty programs and warehouse clubs

Most major chains offer a free loyalty card or app that knocks a few cents off per gallon. Warehouse clubs and some supermarkets offer bigger discounts — sometimes 20 to 30 cents a gallon — if you are already a member. Stack a loyalty discount with a cashback credit card for a double saving.

Pay attention to cash vs. card prices

Some stations charge more for credit cards than for cash. The difference is often 5 to 10 cents a gallon. If you carry cash, the cash price can be the cheapest option at that station.

Keep a simple price log

Note the price at the two or three stations you pass most often. Within a week you will know which one is usually cheapest, and you will spot price jumps quickly. Combine this habit with the calculator on our home page to see exactly what a trip costs before you leave.

What not to do

  • Do not drive far out of your way for a small saving — the fuel you burn cancels it out.
  • Do not let your tank run nearly empty while hunting for a deal; running out of gas is far more expensive than a few cents a gallon.
  • Do not trust a price sign that is not dated — always check the pump price before you start filling.
