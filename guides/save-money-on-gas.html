10 Ways to Save Money on Gas

You do not need a new car or a magic device to spend less on gas. These ten habits cost nothing and can cut a typical driver's fuel bill by 10 to 20 percent.

  1. Drive smoothly. Gentle acceleration and steady speed use far less fuel than hard starts and sudden braking. On the highway, cruise control helps a lot.
  2. Slow down a little. Most cars are most efficient between about 50 and 60 mph. Above 60 mph, fuel use climbs quickly.
  3. Keep your tires inflated. Under-inflated tires waste fuel and wear out faster. Check pressure monthly, when tires are cold.
  4. Remove extra weight. A roof box or a trunk full of things you do not need adds drag and weight. Take off roof racks when you are not using them.
  5. Combine errands. A cold engine uses more fuel, so one trip with several stops beats three separate short trips.
  6. Turn off the engine when parked. Idling for more than a minute burns fuel for nothing. Restarting a modern car uses less fuel than idling.
  7. Use the right gas. If your car is designed for regular 87 octane, premium adds cost and no benefit. Check your manual.
  8. Use air conditioning wisely. At highway speeds, air conditioning is more efficient than open windows. In slow city traffic, open windows can be cheaper.
  9. Keep up with maintenance. A dirty air filter, old spark plugs, and worn oil all hurt fuel economy. Follow your service schedule.
  10. Plan your route. A slightly longer route with steady traffic can use less fuel than a shorter one with constant stop-and-go congestion.
