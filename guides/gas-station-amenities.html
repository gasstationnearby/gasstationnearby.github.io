Gas Station Amenities Explained

Modern gas stations offer far more than fuel. Here is what each amenity does, when it is worth using, and what to check before you rely on it.

EV charging

Many stations now have fast chargers for electric cars. A DC fast charger can add a large amount of range in 20 to 40 minutes, while a slower Level 2 charger is meant for longer stops. Check the charger's power rating (in kW) and whether it fits your car's plug type before you plan a stop around it.

Diesel pumps

Diesel is a separate fuel for diesel engines — never put it in a gasoline car, and never put gasoline in a diesel car. Diesel pumps are usually marked with a green or yellow handle in the US. Larger stations and truck stops almost always carry diesel; small urban stations sometimes do not.

Car wash

Most stations offer an automatic car wash, often at a discount when you buy fuel. Touchless washes are gentler on paint than brush washes. Avoid a wash in freezing weather, when water can freeze on door seals and locks.

Free and paid air

Tire air pumps are common, sometimes free and sometimes coin-operated. Check your tire pressure when the tires are cold — the correct pressure is on a sticker inside your driver's door, not on the tire itself. Proper pressure improves fuel economy and safety.

Restrooms and food

On long trips, a clean restroom and a place to buy water or snacks matter. Larger travel centers usually have the best facilities, plus seating areas. Smaller stations may have a restroom only for customers — a fuel receipt usually gets you access.

Vacuum and other extras

Many stations have coin vacuums, and some offer windshield-washing stations and trash bins. A few larger sites have Wi-Fi, ATMs, and even showers for truck drivers. If you travel regularly, pick a chain whose stations consistently have the amenities you use most.
