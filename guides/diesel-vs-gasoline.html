Diesel vs Gasoline — What to Know

Diesel and gasoline look similar at the pump, but they are different fuels for different engines — and mixing them up is an expensive mistake.

The basic difference

Gasoline engines use spark plugs to ignite a mix of fuel and air. Diesel engines compress air until it is hot enough to ignite the fuel on its own, with no spark plug. Diesel fuel contains more energy per gallon, which is why diesel vehicles often get better mileage and stronger low-speed pulling power.

Fuel economy and cost

GasolineDiesel

Engine maintenanceUsually simplerMore expensive parts

Diesel cars often cost more to buy and service, so the fuel saving only pays off if you drive a lot of miles each year — typically over 15,000.

What happens if you use the wrong fuel?

Putting gasoline in a diesel car can damage the fuel pump and injectors, because diesel also lubricates those parts and gasoline does not. Putting diesel in a gasoline car will make it smoke, run badly, and stall. If you realize your mistake before starting the engine, do not turn the key — call for help and have the tank drained. It is far cheaper than an engine repair.

Which one should you choose?

  • Choose gasoline if you drive mostly short city trips, want lower purchase and service costs, and like a wide choice of models.
  • Choose diesel if you drive long distances, tow heavy loads, or need strong low-end torque for work.
